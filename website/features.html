YOUR MATERIAL. YOUR QUESTIONS.

Study with your AI client.

Let a compatible desktop AI client read your material through an optional MCP connection. Ask it to explain a page, connect ideas across a chapter or quiz you on a notebook.

  • Choose what is shared. Give access to the current page or one selected notebook. Page access follows your active page, including the active side of split screen; notebook access stays tied to the chosen book.
  • Text and visual context. The client can read typed text and PDF text, search available page text, and request page images with handwriting, pictures and PDF annotations.
  • Read-only access. The client cannot edit your notebooks through this connection. Locked pages are excluded, and access turns off when Openote restarts.
  • Bring your own client. There is no built-in model. Local MCP support is required; a browser-only chat cannot reach the local server. Cloud providers process what you send and may charge separately.
How to connect a study client
  1. Open the AI icon at the top right, beside settings.
  2. Enable access and choose the current page or a whole notebook.
  3. Use Copy for chat, paste the setup instructions into your compatible desktop client, and complete its MCP configuration. Reconnect the client if needed.
  4. Keep Openote open and ask, for example, “Explain this page, then ask me three questions about it.”

The setup text contains a secret connection token. Keep it private. Handwriting is provided as images; it does not become automatically searchable OCR text.

A PLACE FOR EVERY SUBJECT

Notebooks, sections, pages.

  • Local notebooks. Create books for subjects, projects or a whole semester. Their contents are stored locally without a required account or cloud server.
  • Sections and page trees. Divide a book into topics, then organize pages and subpages within them. Drag sections and pages into your own order.
  • A searchable library. Find a notebook by name, choose its cover color and reopen it from the notebook library.
  • Import and export. Restore Openote backups or import supported files. Export a page in an offered format, or save a whole notebook as folders and files.
How to organize a course
  1. Open notebook management and choose New.
  2. Create a section for a topic and add its pages.
  3. Drag items to reorder them. To make a subpage, hold a page over another for about half a second, then release when its preview appears. Move away to cancel.
  4. To export a book, right-click one of its pages, open Export, and choose Save the whole notebook as folders and files….

For moving or restoring your complete Openote data, use a notebook backup. The open folder export is a separate option for taking the material elsewhere.

LESS BETWEEN THE SOURCE AND YOUR NOTES

Capture it. Keep working.

  • Direct screen clips. Select a region of your screen and insert it straight into the current page. Capture an example from a website without first saving and then importing an image.
  • Android camera companion. Pair by QR code, scan a worksheet with your phone and send it to the selected Windows page. Your laptop does not need a camera.
  • Phone files, too. Choose an existing phone file instead of a camera scan and transfer it through the same pairing flow.
  • Pictures, PDFs and attachments. Insert material from your computer, combine it on a page and keep the source files available. Clipboard paste and file drops offer other ways to bring material in.
  • Arrange your material. Select multiple images or PDFs and use View → Arrange to lay them out vertically or horizontally.
How to capture part of a website
  1. Open the destination page in Openote.
  2. In Insert, choose Screen (the screen clip control).
  3. Select the region you want to capture. Openote inserts the captured image into your page.
How to send a scan from your phone
  1. On the destination Windows page, choose Scan from phone and leave the pairing window open.
  2. Open the Android companion. Choose scanning or a phone file, then scan the QR code displayed by Openote.
  3. Capture the document or select a file. It arrives below the page’s existing content.

Both devices need a reachable local network, usually the same Wi-Fi. Allow private incoming connections through the Windows firewall. Closing the pairing window stops the receiver.

See the Android app and download the APK.

MADE FOR THE PEN IN YOUR HAND

Write, mark, erase.

  • Pressure-aware pen. Pen strokes respond to pressure when your device reports it. Ballpoint offers a more even line; the highlighter marks important material.
  • Two ways to erase. Erase an area precisely or remove a whole stroke. Adjust the eraser size for the job.
  • Selection and drawing tools. Select ink to move or recolor it. Use the ruler and optional shape recognition when a diagram needs straighter geometry.
  • Touch navigation and palm rejection. Pan and zoom with touch; Windows palm rejection gives an active pen priority. Finger drawing can be set to Auto, Always or Never.
  • Paper backgrounds. Choose blank, grid, dotted or ruled backgrounds to suit writing, diagrams or calculations.
  • Undo and redo. Step back from a mark or restore it while you work.
How to set up your writing tools
  1. Open Draw, select pen, ballpoint or highlighter, then choose color and size.
  2. Choose the eraser and select area or whole-stroke behavior.
  3. Open View → Background for blank, grid, dotted or ruled paper.
  4. In Settings → Writing and drawing → Draw with finger, choose Auto, Always or Never. Auto lets the pen take over.

Pen behavior depends on your hardware and driver. Pressure support does not mean every device or every brush behaves identically.

CHOOSE THE SHAPE OF YOUR WORK

One page. Three ways to work.

Canvas

A boundless workspace for arranging handwriting, text and documents freely. Useful when an idea needs more space than a sheet.

Pages

A defined paper size: A4, A5, A3, Letter, Legal or Tabloid, with a Landscape option. The page layout is chosen per Openote page, so a notebook can contain different kinds of work.

PDF editor

A focused, centered view of imported PDF sheets, stacked for reading and annotation. Work on the PDF sheets rather than spreading them across a boundless canvas. This option appears when the current page contains PDF sheets.

  • Read and annotate PDFs. Import PDFs, select available PDF text and write or highlight on the sheets. This is annotation, not a promise to rewrite the original PDF’s text.
  • Share the result. Export a page as PDF, including a vector PDF or a picture of the page, or save the original source separately.
How to switch page modes
  1. Open View.
  2. Use Canvas / Pages to switch between a boundless canvas and fixed paper. In Pages mode, open Paper size to choose a size or toggle Landscape.
  3. For a focused PDF workspace, first import PDF sheets through Insert → PDF.
  4. Open View → PDF editor. While active, the toggle is labeled PDF slides; use it again to return to the regular workspace.

If the PDF editor control is absent, check that this Openote page actually contains imported PDF sheets.

THE SOURCE AND YOUR WORK, TOGETHER

Two pages, side by side.

  • Independent navigation. Open pages side by side, including pages from different notebooks.
  • Read here, write there. Keep a source document or lecture material beside your notes, calculations or exercise page.
  • Use the right page mode. Combine split screen with each page’s own layout, including a PDF-focused page when PDF sheets are present.
How to open a second page
  1. Open View → Split screen.
  2. Activate the pane you want to change, then navigate to the desired notebook and page in that pane.
  3. Keep your reference in one pane and work in the other.
  4. Use Close split screen to return to a single workspace.

TYPE, PLAN, FIND, KEEP GOING

The rest of your study toolkit.

  • Text and formatting. Type and format notes alongside handwriting with the Home tools.
  • Equations, tables and boards. Insert formulas, tables, code, task boards and page links. Pictures, files and videos can stay with the material they belong to.
  • Search. Use notebook search to find a book and the page search control (Ctrl + F) to find available text on the page.
  • Planner. Keep homework, tasks, reminders and exam dates in the calendar. Choose a calendar day when creating a new entry.
  • Local spelling support. Enable spell check and optional handwriting checks using Windows writing services. Supported Windows language packs are required; recognition can misread handwriting and the marks are suggestions.
  • Light and dark themes. Choose the look in settings. The default ink swatch with the small sparkle symbol adapts between light and dark paper; custom fixed colors keep the color you chose.
How to use the theme-aware ink
  1. Open Draw and select your pen or ballpoint.
  2. Choose the default color swatch marked with the small sparkle symbol. It can look dark or light depending on the theme.
  3. Switch theme in settings. This marked ink adapts; the behavior does not apply to every white or black custom color you pick.
How to enable writing checks
  1. Open Settings → Writing and drawing.
  2. Set Writing language and enable Spell check; optionally enable Check handwriting.
  3. Use Check language support. If a required pack is missing, install the selected handwriting language in Windows settings.

These checks run locally through Windows. They do not turn all handwriting into automatically searchable text, and are separate from sharing page images with an AI client.

MAKE YOUR WORK PORTABLE

Save, export, recover.

  • Local saving. Notebook contents are stored on your computer. Writing with local material works offline; you do not need an Openote account.
  • Complete backups. Back up one or several notebooks to a ZIP and restore through Import. Keep another copy on a separate drive.
  • Export choices. PDF, picture PDF, Markdown, JSON Canvas, InkML and a whole-notebook folder export are available from the page Export menu.
  • Recycle bin. Restore deleted notebooks and deleted sections or pages within their retention period. The retention period is configurable; the bin is not a substitute for a backup.
  • Limited OneNote import. Try local .one and .onepkg files with a build containing the native Rust core. Not all content or layouts are supported. Check the import and keep your originals; there is no direct OneDrive connection.
  • Update when you are ready. All apps check GitHub for newer releases. Windows and Android ask before installation; macOS and Linux open the release page for a manual update.
How to back up, export or restore
  1. For a complete backup, open notebook management, choose Backup, select books and save the ZIP.
  2. To restore a backup or try an imported file, use Import in notebook management.
  3. To share a particular page, right-click that page, choose Export, select a format and pick the destination.
  4. For a recently deleted item, open the sidebar’s Recycle bin and restore it before its retention period expires. Review the period under Settings → Recycle bin retention.
